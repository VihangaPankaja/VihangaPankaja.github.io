Start with the meaning

Membership μA(x) is how well x fits a label. It is not the probability that x belongs. State the universe and preserve its element order.

  • Standard operations: union=max, intersection=min, complement=1−μ.
  • Same universe: combine grades at the same element.
  • Two variables: use ordered pairs and a relation on X×Y.
  • Hedges: very=μ²; more or less=√μ. They change grades, not elements.

Cuts and routes

A weak λ-cut keeps μ≥λ; a strong cut keeps μ>λ. The weak zero-cut is the entire universe. Positive support uses μ>0; the core uses μ=1.

For R:X→Y and S:Y→Z, take one R row and one S column. For each intermediate y, take min or product of the two links. Then take max across routes. The result has rows X and columns Z.

Keep the inference stages separate

Fuzzify inputs → combine antecedent grades → form each rule output → combine outputs. AND inside a rule and aggregation across rules are separate choices.

SystemWhat each rule produces → final output
MamdaniFuzzy set → clip or scale → aggregate → defuzzify.
Sugeno / TSKConstant or affine function value → firing-weighted average.
TsukamotoInvert a strictly monotonic consequent at its firing grade → firing-weighted average.
ANFISA TSK network. Fit consequents with premises fixed; update premises with consequents fixed.

TSK/Tsukamoto need Σα>0. A Mamdani centroid needs positive aggregate area. With no active output, the formula is undefined.

Which scalar is the question asking for?

  • Maximum selection: choose a global maximizer; it may be non-unique.
  • Centroid: first moment divided by area of the aggregate.
  • Continuous MOM: average over the maximizing region.
  • Height-weighted average: weight component centroids by peak heights.
  • Center of sums: weight component centroids by areas; overlaps count in each component.
  • Largest-area center: use the centroid of the largest selected region.

Quick recall

For maxima [2,4]∪{6}, is continuous MOM 3 or 6?

3. The isolated point 6 has zero measure. Choosing 6 belongs to maximum selection.

Do Zadeh and Dienes–Rescher mean the same fuzzy operation?

No. At a=0.5,b=1, max(min(a,b),1−a)=0.5 but max(1−a,b)=1.

Why can clipping and scaling give different centroids?

Scaling preserves relative shape; clipping flattens its top. An asymmetric set makes that difference visible.
