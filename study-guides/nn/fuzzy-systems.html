Mamdani, Sugeno, Tsukamoto and ANFIS

Mamdani, Sugeno and Tsukamoto share fuzzy rule firing. Their consequent types determine how the final number is calculated.

Ask what comes out of each rule

  • Mamdani: a fuzzy output set. Clip or scale it, aggregate the rule sets, then defuzzify.
  • Sugeno / TSK: a constant or an input function. Average the rule values with firing weights.
  • Tsukamoto: invert a strictly monotonic consequent to obtain each rule value, then take the firing-weighted average.

Use fuzzy propositions for rule language and membership and defuzzification for scalar methods. This page compares complete inference systems.

From expert rules to a canonical rule base

An IF–THEN rule connects an antecedent condition with a consequent. Rules encode an expert’s empirical mapping, such as “high temperature → open the cooling valve more.” A physical energy balance instead explains the process through a model.

Reduce a compound antecedent to one firing grade. Standard AND takes min; standard OR takes max. The consequent can then be a fuzzy set, a crisp function or an invertible monotonic set.

Rs:extIFxextisAsextTHENyextisBs,qquads=1,ldots,r

Under min/max operators, AND is limited by the weakest condition and OR by the strongest alternative. For mixed expressions, evaluate the grouped expression tree from the leaves upward.

μA∩B(x)=min{μA(x),μB(x)},μA∪B(x)=max{μA(x),μB(x)}

(A₁ AND A₂) OR A₃ gives 0.80 for grades 0.40,0.60,0.80. A₁ AND (A₂ OR A₃) gives 0.40. The grouping changes the result.

Example 7.4: preserve the source grouping

Mixed antecedent: bottom-up rule evaluation

Source rule: IF (x₁ is A₁ AND x₂ is A₂) OR x₃ is A₃ THEN y is B.
Grades: μA₁(x₁)=0.40, μA₂(x₂)=0.60, μA₃(x₃)=0.80.
Chosen operators: AND=min; OR=max; Mamdani implication=min.

1. Read the grouping before calculating

Rule: (A₁ AND A₂) OR A₃. The lecture convention gives AND higher precedence, so the root is OR and its left child is AND. Keep the parentheses; they determine the tree.

2. Evaluate the AND child

Rule: AND uses the minimum here. Substitute the two left-leaf grades: min(0.40, 0.60) = 0.40. The next operation is the root OR.

3. Evaluate the OR root

Rule: OR uses the maximum. Substitute the completed AND result and the right leaf: max(0.40, 0.80) = 0.80. This is the antecedent firing grade.

4. Apply the consequent implication

Under Mamdani minimum implication, μB′(y)=min(0.80, μB(y)): clip the consequent at height 0.80. This is an output-set operation, not another antecedent connective.

5. Change only the grouping and compare

For A₁ AND (A₂ OR A₃), first max(0.60, 0.80)=0.80, then min(0.40, 0.80)=0.40. The distinct answer proves why the tree must be preserved. The original grouping gives 0.80; the alternate grouping gives 0.40.

The source also gives a temperature-and-pressure rule: “high temperature AND high pressure → valve slightly open.” Grades 0.80 and 0.60 give a min firing grade of 0.60, so Mamdani implication clips “slightly open” at 0.60. Replacing AND with OR gives 0.80.

A crisp centroid cannot be calculated from that example because the consequent membership function and output universe are not specified.

Rule aggregation is separate from antecedent logic

Keep two levels separate:

  • Antecedent logic combines conditions inside one rule.
  • Aggregation combines outputs from different rules.

Conjunctive aggregation intersects outputs; disjunctive aggregation unions them. Min and max are the standard choices here.

μjoint(y)=miniμBi(y),μat least one(y)=maxiμBi(y)

Mamdani has four named stages: fuzzification, rule evaluation/implication, aggregation and defuzzification. Counting firing and implication separately gives five operations. The calculation is the same.

Mamdani: fuzzy consequents, clipping or scaling, then centroid

A rule fires with strength α. Minimum implication clips the consequent at α; product implication scales its grades by α. Aggregate the resulting rule sets with the chosen operator, then defuzzify.

αi=min{μAi(x0),μBi(y0)}
μCi′(z)=min{αi,μCi(z)}(minimum clipping)
μCi′(z)=αiμCi(z)(product scaling)

Clipping flattens the top; scaling shrinks every grade. The shapes and areas differ, so their centroids can differ. Use max aggregation only when specified.

The restaurant example below uses just the continuous centroid requested by the rule system: area under the aggregated output curve supplies the denominator and its first moment supplies the numerator.

Restaurant tip: source functions, rules, and inputs

The restaurant inputs are food=7.5 and service=4.0, with tip in [0,20]%. The two rules are:

  • Delicious OR Good → High.
  • NOT Delicious AND Poor → Low.

The full membership branches and integral breakpoints are in the worked calculation.

Example 7.7: complete restaurant functions and continuous centroid

Restaurant tip: exact continuous Mamdani centroid

Inputs and ranges: food x=7.5 on [0,10]; service y=4 on [0,10]; tip z∈[0,20] percent.
Food: μDel(x)=(x−5)/3 for 5≤x≤8; (10−x)/2 for 8<x≤10; otherwise 0. μNot Del=1−μDel.
Service: μPoor(y)=1−y/5 for 0≤y≤5, else 0; μGood(y)=y/5 for 0≤y≤5 and 1 for y>5.
Tip: μLow(z)=1−z/10 on 0≤z≤10, else 0; μHigh(z)=(z−10)/10 on 10≤z≤20, else 0.
Rules/operators: R₁: Del OR Good → High; R₂: NOT Del AND Poor → Low. Use max for OR, min for AND, min clipping, max aggregation, and the continuous centroid.

1. Fuzzify food and service

Food at x=7.5: it lies on the rising Delicious branch, so μDel=(7.5−5)/3=2.5/3=5/6 and μNot Del=1−5/6=1/6.
Service at y=4: μPoor=1−4/5=1/5 and μGood=4/5=4/5. These four grades are the only input values needed by the rules.

2. Find both rule firing strengths

R₁ uses OR: α₁=max(5/6,4/5)=max(25/30,24/30)=5/6. R₂ uses AND: α₂=min(1/6,1/5)=min(5/30,6/30)=1/6. These strengths set the heights of High and Low under minimum implication.

3. Clip both consequents and locate every breakpoint

Low is clipped at 1/6. Its descending line 1−z/10 reaches that height when 1−z/10=1/6, so z=10(5/6)=25/3. Thus its pieces change at z=0, 25/3, and 10.
High is clipped at 5/6. Its rising line (z−10)/10 reaches that height when z=10+10(5/6)=55/3.

Thus its pieces change at z=10, 55/3, and 20. The complete ordered breakpoint list is 0, 25/3, 10, 55/3, 20.

4. Write the aggregated curve interval by interval

The two supports meet only at z=10, where both have grade 0, so max aggregation does not create an overlap with positive area. The exact aggregate is:
0≤z≤25/3: 1/6; 25/3<z≤10: 1−z/10; 10≤z<55/3: (z−10)/10; 55/3≤z≤20: 5/6; 0 outside [0,20]. At each endpoint the adjacent formulas agree.

5. Low-tip area and first moment

Split at 25/3. Area: AL=(25/3)(1/6)+∫25/310(1−z/10)dz=25/18+5/36=55/36. The second term is the triangle with base 5/3 and height 1/6.
First moment: ML=∫025/3z/6 dz+∫25/310z(1−z/10)dz=625/108+100/81=1875/324+400/324=2275/324. The antiderivative for the second integral is z²/2−z³/30.

6. High-tip area and first moment

Split at 55/3. Area: AH=∫1055/3(z−10)/10 dz+(20−55/3)(5/6)=125/36+25/18=175/36. The ramp triangle has base 25/3 and height 5/6; the last piece is a plateau rectangle.
First moment: MH=∫1055/3z(z−10)/10 dz+∫55/320(5/6)z dz=4375/81+2875/108=17500/324+8625/324=26125/324. Antiderivatives are z³/30−z²/2 and 5z²/12, respectively.

7. Add moments and areas, then take the centroid

Because the two clipped supports overlap only at a zero-height point, max aggregation adds the two areas and moments: A=55/36+175/36=230/36=115/18; M=2275/324+26125/324=28400/324=7100/81.
Therefore z*=M/A=(7100/81)/(115/18)=14200/1035=2840/207≈13.7198068%, or about 13.72%.

8. Check the result independently

Area check: 115/18≈6.38889; moment check: 7100/81≈87.65432. Their ratio is 13.7198. The result lies inside [0,20] and above 10, consistent with the larger high-tip area (175/36 versus 55/36).

The stated Mamdani restaurant system gives a tip of 13.72%.

The detailed calculation separates grades, firing, clipping, aggregation and centroid so the result can be checked.

Sugeno / TSK: crisp constant or input-function consequents

A TSK consequent is a crisp value: a constant in zero-order TSK, or an affine input function in first-order TSK. Evaluate each function at the input, then take its firing-weighted average. There is no output fuzzy-set centroid.

fi(𝐱)=ci(zero order),fi(𝐱)=pi0+∑j=1npijxj(first order)
w¯i=wi∑kwk,y*=∑iw¯ifi(𝐱)=∑iwifi(𝐱)∑iwi

With nonnegative strengths and a positive total, normalized weights sum to 1. The result lies between the active rule values. Zero total strength makes the formula undefined.

Example 7.8 uses x=4,y=2 and consequents z₁=3x+2y+4=20, z₂=2x+5y+12=30. Grade pairs are (0.6,0.8) and (0.5,0.4).

The example does not select an AND operator. Minimum gives strengths 0.6,0.4 and result 24. Product gives a different answer.

Example 7.8: exact TSK values and all five ANFIS inference layers

Two-rule first-order TSK example through the ANFIS layers

Source inputs: x=4, y=2.
Rule consequents: z₁=3x+2y+4; z₂=2x+5y+12.
Membership grades: Rule 1 (0.6,0.8); Rule 2 (0.5,0.4).
Main calculation convention: min T-norm; product alternative shown in the final step.

1. Declare the AND operator and retain the givens

The source lists min and product T-norms but does not specify one for Example 7.8. Use the standard minimum AND for the requested source result. Inputs are x=4, y=2. Rule 1 grades are (0.6,0.8); Rule 2 grades are (0.5,0.4). Consequents: z₁=3x+2y+4 and z₂=2x+5y+12.

2. Layer 1: evaluate memberships

The crisp values have already been fuzzified by the supplied grades: Rule 1 receives 0.6 for A₁ and 0.8 for B₁; Rule 2 receives 0.5 for A₂ and 0.4 for B₂. A full ANFIS would compute these from adaptive membership functions; this source example gives the grades directly.

3. Layer 2: calculate firing strengths

With minimum AND: w₁=min(0.6,0.8)=0.6 and w₂=min(0.5,0.4)=0.4. These nonnegative strengths are the two rule activations.

4. Layer 3: normalize the strengths

Their sum is 0.6+0.4=1. Therefore w̄₁=0.6/1=0.6 and w̄₂=0.4/1=0.4. The normalized values sum to 1, as required for a weighted average.

5. Layer 4: evaluate and weight each consequent

Substitute x=4,y=2: z₁=3(4)+2(2)+4=12+4+4=20; z₂=2(4)+5(2)+12=8+10+12=30. Weighted contributions are w̄₁z₁=0.6(20)=12 and w̄₂z₂=0.4(30)=12.

6. Layer 5: sum the contributions and check

z*=12+12=24. Independent check: normalized weights total 1, and 20≤24≤30, so the result is a valid convex combination of the two rule outputs.

7. Compare the other stated T-norm

If product AND is chosen instead, w₁=0.6(0.8)=0.48 and w₂=0.5(0.4)=0.20. Then z*=[0.48(20)+0.20(30)]/(0.48+0.20)=15.6/0.68=390/17≈22.9412. The method is unchanged, but the numeric result changes. State the T-norm whenever a problem does not specify it.

ANFIS: five TSK layers and hybrid parameter fitting

ANFIS embeds first-order TSK in five layers: memberships, firing strengths, normalized strengths, weighted consequent values and their sum. Under minimum AND, the TSK example gives 0.6/0.4 → 0.6/0.4 → 12/12 → 24 after the membership layer.

LayerOperationWhat adaptsTSK example state
1Fuzzify inputsPremise membership parameters0.6, 0.8; 0.5, 0.4
2Compute rule firingSelected T-normw₁=0.6; w₂=0.4
3Normalize firing valuesNo new parameterw̄₁=0.6; w̄₂=0.4
4Evaluate each weighted consequentLinear consequent coefficients12 and 12
5Sum the weighted valuesNo new parameterz*=24

Hybrid learning alternates between two parameter groups:

  • Forward pass: fix premise parameters and fit linear consequent parameters by least squares.
  • Backward pass: fix consequents and update nonlinear premise parameters by gradient descent.

Least squares solves the fixed-premise linear subproblem. The whole model is still nonlinear.

Tsukamoto: monotonic consequents map firing grades to crisp values

Tsukamoto requires strictly monotonic consequent membership functions. Invert each at its firing grade to get one crisp rule output, then take the firing-weighted average. A non-monotonic consequent can give several inverse values.

yi=μCi−1(αi),y*=∑iαiyi∑iαi

The restaurant High ramp μ(y)=y/20 gives y₁=20α₁. The Low ramp μ(y)=(20−y)/20 gives y₂=20(1−α₂). These are crisp inverse outputs, not Mamdani clipped curves.

Example 7.9: inverse consequent outputs and weighted tip

Tsukamoto restaurant: fire, invert, average

Inputs: food 7.5, service 4.0; [0,10] scales. Food grades 5/6 and 1/6; service grades Good=4/5 and Poor=1/5.
Rules: Del OR Good → High; NOT Del AND Poor → Low.
Consequents: μHigh(y)=y/20; μLow(y)=(20−y)/20 on [0,20].

1. Reuse the source membership grades

At x₁=7.5, μDel=5/6 and μNot Del=1/6. At x₂=4, μGood=4/5 and μPoor=1/5. The antecedent functions are the same ones used in the Mamdani restaurant example.

2. Fire both rules with their own connective

Rule 1 uses OR, so α₁=max(5/6,4/5)=5/6. Rule 2 uses AND, so α₂=min(1/6,1/5)=1/6. These grades become inverse-mapping levels for the two monotonic output sets.

3. Invert the High and Low consequents

High: μHigh(y)=y/20=α₁ gives y₁=20(5/6)=50/3. Low: μLow(y)=(20−y)/20=α₂ gives y₂=20(1−1/6)=20(5/6)=50/3. The outputs coincide.

4. Average and independently check

y*=[(5/6)(50/3)+(1/6)(50/3)]/(5/6+1/6)=(50/3)(1)/(1)=50/3≈16.67%. Since both rule outputs equal 50/3 and the firing weights are positive, every normalized weighted average must return that same value.

Example 7.10: exact crisp baseline and fuzzy underdetermination

Heat exchanger: crisp baseline and missing fuzzy design data

Given: Ps=68.95 kPa; Ts=362.7 K; T₁=17°C=290.15 K; Ts−T₁=72.55 K; Cp=1.7543 kJ/(kg·K); w=1300 kg/s; ΔTapp=6.5 K.

Given steam saturation temperature Ts=362.7 K and inlet T₁=17°C, convert with 17+273.15=290.15 K. Then Ts−T₁=362.7−290.15=72.55 K, matching the source. The approach temperature is ΔTapp=6.5 K.

wCp=1300(1.7543)=2280.59 kJ/(s·K)=2280.59 kW/K. The ratio is 72.55/6.5=11.16153846 and ln(ratio)=2.412473802. Thus AU=2280.59(2.412473802)=5501.8636 kW/K (about 5501.86 kW/K). Pressure Ps=68.95 kPa is given but does not occur in the supplied AU equation.

The source supplies the three rules but no membership functions for large/small flow, small/large approach, or large/small exchanger, and no numeric first-order TSK consequents. Therefore the grades, firing strengths, and fuzzy AU outputs are underdetermined.

T-norms model AND; S-norms model OR. Min and max are standard examples, not the only choices. All inputs below lie in [0,1].

For a T-norm T and S-norm S, the defining conditions include commutativity, associativity, monotonicity, and the boundary identities T(a,1)=a and S(a,0)=a. Standard complement is N(a)=1−a, and De Morgan duality gives S(a,b)=1−T(1−a,1−b). The source also lists Sugeno and Yager complements and the Yager T-norm family:

N(a)=1−a,Ns(a)=1−a1+sa (s>−1),Nw(a)=(1−aw)1/w (w>0)

As p→∞, this Yager T-norm approaches minimum. The source names compensatory averages, weighted generalized means, and ordered weighted averaging (OWA), with the bound min(a,b)≤M(a,b)≤max(a,b). Its printed weighted-mean expression does not specify weight normalization or valid parameter/exception cases, so treat it as a family name until those assumptions are supplied.

Correct pointwise hierarchy

Source correction: Appendix A.5 reverses some operator bounds. Use the corrected pointwise chains below:

TD≤TL≤TP≤TM=minandSM=max≤SP≤SL≤SD

Here TD is drastic product, TL Łukasiewicz, TP algebraic product, and TM minimum. SM is maximum, SP algebraic sum, SL bounded sum, and SD drastic sum. For arbitrary valid operators, the bounds are TD≤T≤min(a,b)≤max(a,b)≤S≤SD.

Thus max(a,b) is a lower bound on every S-norm, not an upper bound. The correct generic T-norm and S-norm bounds do not require the two chosen operators to be dual; named dual operators pair by the standard complement.

The correction follows the pointwise order in the primary treatment by Klement, Mesiar, and Pap: Triangular norms, Position paper I, especially its smallest/largest t-norm statement and four basic t-norm order; the bounds on min and max are also proved in Klement, Mesiar, and Pap, Triangular Norms and Conorms.

ANFIS’s hybrid split is consistent with Jang’s primary paper, ANFIS: Adaptive-Network-Based Fuzzy Inference System.

Common mistakes

  • Do not flatten or regroup a mixed antecedent; evaluate its actual tree bottom-up. Here the source tree returns 0.80, while the alternate grouping returns 0.40.
  • Do not confuse antecedent AND/OR with the later aggregation across multiple rule consequents.
  • Do not call minimum clipping and product scaling the same Mamdani implication.
  • Do not send a TSK function output through Mamdani centroid defuzzification; TSK uses a weighted average of crisp rule values.
  • Do not use Tsukamoto unless every output membership is strictly monotonic over the stated range and can be inverted uniquely.
  • Do not normalize weights without checking the total is positive.
  • Do not claim ANFIS’s whole model is linear: only its consequent parameters are linear when premises are fixed.
  • Do not infer heat-exchanger fuzzy outputs from the crisp AU equation alone; the required membership functions and TSK consequent data are absent.
  • Do not repeat the Appendix’s reversed hierarchy: every T-norm is ≤ minimum, and every S-norm is ≥ maximum.

Exam focus

  • Define antecedent/consequent and reduce canonical fuzzy-set antecedents with the stated min/max operators.
  • Draw the mixed connective tree, honor precedence/grouping, and show each intermediate grade.
  • Distinguish within-rule logic from conjunctive/disjunctive rule aggregation.
  • For Mamdani, show fuzzification, firing, clip or scale, aggregate, and apply the requested defuzzifier.
  • For TSK, state zero-/first-order form, T-norm, raw rule values, normalized weights, weighted sum, and positive-denominator condition.
  • For ANFIS, name all five layers and explain the fixed-premise least-squares forward pass and fixed-consequent premise-gradient backward pass.
  • For Tsukamoto, verify strict monotonicity, invert each consequent, then calculate the firing-weighted average.
  • For an engineering fuzzy case, separate a computable physical baseline from missing fuzzy-design inputs.
  • Recall the correct bounds: TD≤T≤min and max≤S≤SD; named operators refine these chains.

Practice questions

With grades 0.40, 0.60, and 0.80, evaluate (A₁ AND A₂) OR A₃ using min for AND and max for OR. What does A₁ AND (A₂ OR A₃) return?

Check your answer

First grouping: min(0.40,0.60)=0.40, then max(0.40,0.80)=0.80. Alternate grouping: max(0.60,0.80)=0.80, then min(0.40,0.80)=0.40.

At firing strength α=0.3, compare minimum implication and product implication at a consequent grade μ=0.8.

Check your answer

Minimum implication gives min(0.3,0.8)=0.3 (a clip). Product implication gives 0.3×0.8=0.24 (a scaled grade).

Why does the source TSK example return 24 under minimum AND?

Check your answer

Firing weights are 0.6 and 0.4; consequent outputs are 20 and 30. They already sum to 1, so the weighted result is 0.6(20)+0.4(30)=24.

What condition on Tsukamoto output memberships makes inverse mapping valid?

Check your answer

Each consequent membership function must be strictly monotonic over the stated output range, so a firing grade maps to a unique crisp output.

Which parameters are held fixed during each ANFIS hybrid pass?

Check your answer

Forward: premise parameters are fixed while consequent parameters are fitted by least squares. Backward: consequent parameters are fixed while nonlinear premise parameters are updated by gradient descent.

Can the source heat-exchanger rules produce unique Mamdani, TSK, or Tsukamoto outputs as printed?

Check your answer

No. Antecedent and output membership functions are not supplied, and TSK consequent coefficients/constants are missing. The crisp baseline is calculable, but the fuzzy values are underdetermined.

What is the correct universal relationship between an arbitrary S-norm and maximum?

Check your answer

For all a,b in [0,1], max(a,b)≤S(a,b)≤S_D(a,b). Maximum is the smallest S-norm; drastic sum is the largest.
