Crisp and fuzzy sets

How much does this value fit the label?

  • State the universe: every grade belongs to a particular element in X.
  • Read μA(x) as fit: 0.7 membership in “hot” is not a 70% probability that a temperature is hot.
  • Operate point by point: compare A and B at the same element. Do not add the grades across the universe.

Universe, crisp sets, and fuzzy sets

Start by defining the universe X. Every set and complement is relative to it.

  • x: one element of X.
  • |X|: number of elements for finite X.
  • P(X): all crisp subsets of X.
  • ∅: no elements; X: the whole universe.

Crisp operations before the fuzzy extension

Crisp operations answer four questions: in either set, in both, outside a set, or in A but outside B?

A∪B={x∈X:x∈A or x∈B}
A∩B={x∈X:x∈A and x∈B}
B―=X⧵B
A⧵B=A∩B―

For several sets, union needs membership in at least one of them; intersection needs membership in every one. The source writes set difference with a vertical delimiter; the set-minus notation here has the same meaning.

Crisp-set laws and why they help

Commutativity lets you swap the two sets. Associativity lets you regroup repeated unions or repeated intersections.

A∪B=B∪A
A∩B=B∩A
A∪(B∪C)=(A∪B)∪C
A∩(B∩C)=(A∩B)∩C

Distributivity rewrites a combination of union and intersection. Each expression below can be rewritten as the one immediately after it.

A∪(B∩C)
=(A∪B)∩(A∪C)
A∩(B∪C)
=(A∩B)∪(A∩C)

Idempotence means repeating a set changes nothing. The identity and boundary laws describe combining it with the empty or universal set.

A∪A=A,A∩A=A
A∪∅=A,A∩X=A
A∩∅=∅,A∪X=X

Containment is transitive: if the first set is inside the second, and the second inside the third, the first is inside the third. Involution means complementing twice recovers the set.

A⊆B, B⊆C ⟹ A⊆C
A――=A

For crisp sets, excluded middle and excluded contradiction say every element either belongs or does not belong, and cannot do both. De Morgan exchanges union and intersection when taking complements.

A∪A―=X,A∩A―=∅
A∩B―=A―∪B―
A∪B―=A―∩B―

In the arch example, E₁ and E₂ mean that each member survives. Both are needed: survival is E₁∩E₂. Collapse is its complement, so De Morgan gives Ē₁∪Ē₂: either failure is enough.

A crisp indicator returns only 0 or 1. Crisp sets are the endpoint-only special case of membership grades in [0,1].

Membership means degree of fit, not probability

A fuzzy set assigns one definite grade μA(x) to every x once the function is chosen. The grade measures fit to the concept, not the probability of belonging.

μA:X→[0,1],x↦μA(x)

The lecture’s two “old” curves agree at newborn→0, age 40→0.5 and ages ≥120→1. Their intermediate shapes differ. The label alone does not specify a unique curve, and no exact intermediate equation is supplied.

A separate “young” set may also assign age 40 a grade of 0.5.

Writing a fuzzy set

In finite-set notation, 0.7/30 pairs grade 0.7 with element 30. The slash is a separator, and plus signs collect pairs. They are not division and addition. The continuous integral-style notation is also a collection, not an arithmetic integral.

A~={∑i=1nμA(xi)xi}
A~={∫XμA(x)x}

On a universe X, the empty fuzzy set has grade 0 everywhere and the whole fuzzy set has grade 1 everywhere. The fuzzy power set P̃(X) collects all fuzzy sets on X. Pointwise containment means every element’s grade in the first set is no greater than its grade in the second.

μ∅(x)=0,μX(x)=1

These values and the following containment comparison apply to every element of the universe.

A~⊆B~⟺μA(x)≤μB(x)

Standard fuzzy-set operations

A one-element check explains why excluded middle and contradiction do not generally carry over.
At one element xRuleFor A=0.4, B=0.7
Union A∪Bmax(A,B)0.7
Intersection A∩Bmin(A,B)0.4
Complement Aᶜ1−A0.6
A∪Aᶜmax(A,1−A)0.6, not 1
A∩Aᶜmin(A,1−A)0.4, not 0

The lecture uses max for union, min for intersection and 1−μ for complement. Apply each operation at the same element of the universe.

μA∪B(x)=max{μA(x),μB(x)}μA∩B(x)=min{μA(x),μB(x)}μA―(x)=1−μA(x)

The standard operations retain commutativity, associativity, distributivity, idempotence, identity, involution, De Morgan and transitive containment.

Excluded middle and contradiction can fail. At μ=0.4, union with its complement is max(0.4,0.6)=0.6, while intersection is min(0.4,0.6)=0.4. At crisp grades 0 and 1, the classical identities hold.

Common mistakes

  • Do not read a grade such as 0.7 as a 70% probability. It measures fit to the chosen fuzzy concept.
  • Do not interpret the slash in finite-set notation as division, or the plus signs as addition.
  • With the lecture’s operators, union is max and intersection is min; do not substitute sum or product.
  • Complement is 1 − μA(x), evaluated separately for each x.
  • Do not confuse the universal set X with its power set P(X). The latter contains subsets of X.
  • Excluded middle and excluded contradiction are not general fuzzy identities; a counterexample shows possible failure, not failure at every element.

Exam focus

  • Define a fuzzy set by a membership function from the universe to [0, 1].
  • Recognize crisp sets as the 0/1-valued special case.
  • Apply max, min, and 1 − μ pointwise, preserving the universe’s element order.
  • State fuzzy containment using a grade comparison for every element.
  • Distinguish laws that carry over (including De Morgan’s laws) from excluded middle and contradiction, which fail in general.

Practice questions

If μA(x) = 0.25 and μB(x) = 0.6, what are the grades of A ∪ B, A ∩ B, and complement A?

Check your answer

Union is max(0.25, 0.6) = 0.6; intersection is min(0.25, 0.6) = 0.25; complement A is 1 − 0.25 = 0.75.

Does μA(x) = 0.7 mean there is a 70% chance that x belongs to A?

Check your answer

No. It is a degree of fit to the modeled concept, not a probability. Probability and fuzzy membership represent different ideas.

What is the difference between X and P(X)?

Check your answer

X contains the universe elements. P(X) contains all subsets of X; the fuzzy power set contains all fuzzy sets on X.

Why is A ∪ complement A not necessarily the whole fuzzy set?

Check your answer

At a partial grade such as 0.4, the union grade is max(0.4, 0.6) = 0.6, below 1. The identity therefore fails in general, though it holds at crisp grades.
