Interval heap

Store a small interval at every node. The low endpoints form a min heap and the high endpoints form a max heap.

Rule and representationOperations and exact namesSpecial operations, caveats and exam focusInsertion decisions and a 12-record traceMethod choices and stop conditionsCourse layout variant and exam checks

Rule and representation

Every node has [lo,hi] with lo ≤ hi. A child's interval must lie inside its parent's interval. The complete tree's last node may hold one item x, interpreted as [x,x] for comparisons. The root exposes both extremes in O(1).

In the lab's 1-based element array, node i uses positions 2i−1 and 2i. Do not confuse node indexes with element indexes.

Build from a fresh list [8, 3, 10, 1, 6]

Input list[8, 3, 10, 1, 6]

1 · Empty, then read 8

With n=0, create one physical root item; its low and high comparisons are the same value, and no parent repair is needed.

2 · Read 3

Sort the two distinct records into [3,8]; this restores local endpoint order, and root has no parent, so insertion stops.

3 · Read 10

Compare 10 with parent high 8; because it is larger, move the old high 8 to the new singleton and bubble 10 upward until root or an adequate parent.

4 · Read 1

Pair 1 with 8 as [1,8], then compare low 1 against parent low 3; exchange lows and continue only while the next ancestor low is larger.

5 · Read 6

The singleton 6 is inside parent interval [3,8], so both low and high heap orders hold; equality with either bound also means stop.

Operations and exact names

Method Repair Cost
initialize Order local endpoints, then bottom-up trickleDownMin and trickleDownMax O(n)
insert Fill last node or add singleton; bubbleUpMin or bubbleUpMax O(logn)
getMin / getMax Root endpoints O(1)
removeMin / removeMax Replace from last node, trickle down the relevant endpoint heap O(logn)
search General scan; intervals can prune O(n) worst case
remove(handle) Replace the located endpoint and repair its side and pair O(logn)
meld Concatenate records, initialize again O(n+m)

Repeated insertion above is O(nlogn). The bulk initialize method is O(n). A singleton still needs both the parent's low and high comparisons.

RemoveMin: include the within-node swap

1 · Start

For n=6, return root low 1, remove the final node's low 8, and retain singleton 15; hold 8 in the root low hole and keep the tree complete.

2 · Move smaller child low upward

Compare child lows 3 and 8, promote 3, and carry the hole down. Before continuing, compare held 8 with that node's high 5 because the pair could invert.

3 · Repair the child interval

Since 8 exceeds 5, put 8 at the high endpoint and carry 5 into the low hole; the node is a leaf, so stop after verifying parent containment.

RemoveMax is the mirror operation

1 · Original example

Return root high 10 and replace it with last singleton 6; the root high hole must be repaired by the largest child high, 8.

2 · Promote larger child high

Promote high 8 and hold 6 at the child high hole; compare 6 with the child's low 3 before continuing, because a too-small high would reverse the pair.

3 · Fill the remaining hole

Here 6 is at least 3, so the pair [3,6] is ordered and the leaf ends the sift. If held value were below low, swap endpoints and carry the displaced low.

Special operations, caveats and exam focus

Complementary range search reports records outside [a,b]. A contained node interval prunes its entire subtree. Follow low endpoints for values < a and high endpoints for values > b. The lecture's O(k) reporting bound includes constant setup, so write O(k+1) when k can be zero. Repeated destructive removeMin/removeMax would instead cost O(klogn).

Handle n=0 as empty, n=1 as the only item, and n=2 as one pair before the general removal logic. Fixing low/high order after downward movement is essential. Duplicate endpoints are valid. A pair [17,17] can represent two different items.

Check your answer

Move a replacement toward the leaves, choosing the smaller child low for removeMin or the larger child high for removeMax, while keeping each node’s endpoints ordered.

Insertion decisions and a 12-record trace

Lecture 3 illustrates a binary interval heap for the diagrams and Lab 5 numbers endpoint slots as 2i−1 and 2i for node i. A node that contains one final record is still one physical item; the interval notation [x,x] is only for comparisons. The array is breadth-first, so the last node is either a singleton or the rightmost full pair.

For a completed pair, compare its low and high endpoints with the corresponding parent endpoints and bubble only the violated side. A newly added singleton is tested against both parent ends: below the parent low means a min-side bubble, above the parent high means a max-side bubble, and inside the interval means stop. Equality is valid and stops. When the prior last node is a singleton, the new record first completes and sorts that pair; it cannot violate both parent ends because the old singleton was already contained.

Insert all records from [12, 5, 22, 3, 18, 27, 8, 31, 14, 25, 1, 10]

Input list[12, 5, 22, 3, 18, 27, 8, 31, 14, 25, 1, 10]

Insert 12

The root starts as one physical item; with no parent, stop.

Insert 5

Sort the two root endpoints. The pair now has low ≤ high, and the root has no ancestor to check.

Insert 22

The new singleton 22 exceeds its parent high 12, so swap on the max side. The displaced 12 is the child singleton; stop at the root.

Insert 3

Complete the last node as [3,12], then compare lows: 3 < 5. Promote 3 and leave 5 in the child; the parent interval still contains the child.

Insert 18

The new singleton is between the root endpoints 3 and 22. Neither endpoint heap is violated, so stop without bubbling.

Insert 27

Pair 27 with 18 to make [18,27]. Its high 27 exceeds the root high 22, so bubble only the high endpoint and stop at the root.

Insert 8

The singleton 8 lies inside parent [5,12], so both endpoint orders hold. Equality at either bound would also stop.

Insert 31

Pair 31 with 8 as [8,31]. Its parent is node 2, [5,12], so exchange highs 31 and 12: node 4 becomes [8,12] and node 2 becomes [5,31]. Continue upward: 31 also exceeds root high 27, so exchange those highs. The root ends as [3,31] and node 2 as [5,27]. Stop at the root.

Insert 14

The new singleton 14 fits inside parent [5,27]. Stop here; no endpoint can move upward.

Insert 25

Complete the final pair as [14,25]. Both endpoints remain inside [5,27], so neither min nor max bubbling starts.

Insert 1

The singleton 1 is below parent low 18, then below root low 3. Each low swap moves the displaced value down; stop at the root.

Insert 10

Complete the singleton as [10,18]. It is contained by parent [3,22], so both sides stop; all 12 input records are present.

Method choices and stop conditions

The costs below are worst-case unless marked output-sensitive; no expected or amortized bound is being claimed for these interval-heap operations.

Method Decision at each step Stop condition and edge cases Cost / course boundary
initialize Order each node's endpoints, then build the low min-heap and high max-heap bottom-up, as on Lecture 3 slide 30. Finish when every node has ordered endpoints and both endpoint heaps hold; equality needs no exchange. Empty and singleton inputs need no descent. O(n) bottom-up build stated in the current lab guide; repeated insertion instead costs O(nlogn).
insert(x) Complete the final singleton or append a singleton; select the low or high endpoint path using the parent interval. Stop at root or the first parent endpoint that is ≤ the low / ≥ the high. Inside-parent and equal values stop immediately. O(logn) for the binary course diagram; degree-4 variant still has logarithmic height.
getMin / getMax Read root low / root high. Empty heap returns failure; with one record, both answers are that record. Duplicate extrema are allowed. O(1).
removeMin For n>2, return root low, take the last node's low, delete that node if empty, and sift down the low side. Choose the smaller child low; repair a crossing pair before continuing. n=0 fails; n=1 becomes empty; n=2 returns low and leaves high as a singleton. Stop at a leaf or when the carried value is no greater than the chosen child low and the pair is ordered. O(logn). The mirrored removeMax chooses the larger child high.
removeMax For n>2, return root high, take the last node's high, remove an emptied last node, and sift down the high side. Choose the larger child high and repair any low/high crossing. n=0 fails; n=1 becomes empty; n=2 returns high and leaves low. Stop at a leaf or when the carried value is no less than the selected child high and the pair is ordered. O(logn).
search(x) Test endpoints; if x is outside [lo,hi], discard that whole subtree, otherwise inspect children. Stop on a match, on an empty search frontier, or when every remaining interval excludes x. For all duplicate occurrences, continue after a match. O(n) worst case; interval pruning is supplemental search detail, not a separate lecture cost claim.
remove(handle) The lecture specifies removal by location. Replace that located endpoint with the last record, restore local pair order, then repair the affected endpoint heap. A valid location identifies one occurrence even when keys duplicate. Stop when parent and child endpoint orders hold; a missing/stale handle must not mutate the heap. Value-only lookup first costs O(n). O(logn) given a maintained location/handle, as the lecture states for removing a point by location. The lecture does not define a stable-handle representation.
meld(A,B) Concatenate the records and run initialize on the combined set. Empty input returns the rebuilt other heap; equal keys remain separate records. No pointer-splicing meld is described for interval heaps in this lecture. O(n+m) rebuild from the current lab guide; not a constant-time meld.
Complementary range report For query [a,b], emit a node interval wholly below (hi<a) or above (lo>b) by enumerating its subtree; prune a wholly contained interval (lo≥a and hi≤b); inspect endpoints and recurse only for partial overlap. Inclusive boundaries a and b are inside. Stop at a contained subtree or a leaf. If k=0, setup still costs constant time. Lecture 3 slide 37 states O(k); write O(k+1) when including the zero-output case. Repeated destructive removals cost O(klogn).

Remove minimum from a 12-record interval heap

1 · Remove the root low and take the last low

Return 1. The removed last-node endpoint is 20, held outside the hole. Continue by promoting the smallest child low.

2 · Promote the smaller child low

The held replacement 20 is greater than node 2's high 6. It cannot fill the low slot while preserving lo ≤ hi, so swap it with that high endpoint and carry 6 downward.

3 · Swap across the node, then continue on the low side

Promote child low 4 into node 2's low hole. At leaf node 4, carried 6 exceeds high 5, so swap: the leaf becomes [5,6]. The leaf ends the path; the resulting intervals remain contained by their parents.

4 · Final repaired heap

The remaining keys are 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 18, 20, 25, 30, and 40. The root still exposes minimum 3 and maximum 40.

RemoveMax with two within-node endpoint swaps

This second valid 12-record state makes the replacement too small for the selected pairs. It is a focused repair example; the larger insertion walk above shows construction from a list.

Remove maximum from a 12-record interval heap

1 · Remove the root high and take the last high

Return 40. Hold the removed last-node high 20 and choose the child with larger high endpoint; node 2 has 30, greater than 25.

2 · Promote the larger child high

The held replacement 20 is below node 2's low 28. Swap across the pair to preserve endpoint order, then continue with 28 on the high side.

3 · Swap across both affected pairs

Stop at the leaf. Every child interval is contained in its parent; the remaining maximum is the root high 30, while root low 0 remains the minimum.

Complementary range report example

For [8,20], the root [1,40] is partial, so test its endpoints and descend. The left subtree rooted at [3,6] is wholly below the range; report its six records and do not test its descendants. The right subtree is partial: keep 18, report 30, then report 25 from [20,25] and both values from [22,28]. The outside multiset is {1, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 22, 25, 28, 30, 40}; 18 and 20 are the two in-range records, so k=12 for this 14-record example.

Course layout variant and exam checks

Lecture 3 slides 12–30 teach interval operations with a binary complete tree; slide 36 then recommends a degree-4 complete tree for cache-aligned interval heaps. The d-ary formulas on slides 33–35 use parent(i)=ceil((i−1)/d) and children d(i−1)+2 through di+1 for breadth-first one-based node numbers. For d=4, compare up to four child lows or highs per descent. The height is about log₄n rather than log₂n, while each level examines more children. Do not apply the Lab 5 binary element-slot formula to the 4-way node layout.

Check before stopping a repair: every node has low≤high; each child low is at least its parent low; each child high is at most its parent high; and the last node alone may be a singleton. A pair [17,17] can contain two distinct equal-key records. A singleton [17] contains only one. Keep multiplicity when reporting or removing equal keys.
