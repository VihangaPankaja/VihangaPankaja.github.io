Correspondence heaps

Split records between a min queue and a max queue, with ordered pairs between them and one buffer for an odd item.

Rule and fresh build

In total correspondence, each min-side record is paired with a distinct max-side record that is at least as large. Each record is stored once. For odd n, one record stays in a buffer.

Build from [8, 3, 6, 1, 5]

Input list[8, 3, 6, 1, 5]

1 · Start empty; read 8

With both queues empty, the first record 8 has no partner, so place it in the single buffer. The representation invariant is satisfied because there are zero pairs and exactly one logical record; continue when another record arrives.

2 · Read 3 and form a pair

Compare 3 with buffered 8. Put the smaller value 3 in the min queue and 8 in the max queue, then link those record identities; 3≤8 establishes total correspondence. Continue only after both heaps and the reciprocal handle are updated.

3 · Read 6

The buffer is empty after forming the pair, so read 6 into the buffer without touching either heap. This keeps each queue at one node and leaves one unmatched logical record; continue on the next input.

4 · Read 1 and form a pair

Compare new value 1 with buffered 6, then place 1 on the min side and 6 on the max side. The two new heap entries satisfy 1≤6 and receive their own link; sift each heap as needed and keep links attached to records.

5 · Read 5

With an empty buffer, put 5 there. The five logical records are the four heap entries plus this buffer value; a peek must compare 5 with the min root for minimum and with the max root for maximum, then stop after returning the winning record.

Operations

Insert into an empty buffer in O(1). If the buffer is occupied, put the smaller item into the min queue, the larger into the max queue, and connect them. With binary heaps this takes O(logn).

The illustrated repeated-insertion build is O(nlogn). For a linear bulk build, form disjoint input pairs, place each pair's smaller item on the min side and larger item on the max side, retain the cross-pair identities, and bottom-up heapify each side while updating pointers. An unpaired item becomes the buffer. This costs O(n).

To removeMin, compare the buffer with the min root. If the buffer wins, clear it. Otherwise remove the min record and its max-side partner, then reinsert that partner through the buffer procedure. removeMax is symmetric. Peek costs O(1), endpoint removal O(logn), search O(n), and a general meld can rebuild in O(n+m).

Remove minimum from the completed example

1 · Select 1

Compare the min root 1 with buffer 5; 1 is smaller, so remove the min-root record and follow its identity link to partner 6. Do not choose the max root as the removed value; continue with both paired records identified.

2 · Remove 1 and temporarily remove partner 6

Delete 1 from the min heap and 6 from its interior max-heap position using arbitrary removal. Repair each affected heap and update every moved record handle; retain partner 6 as pending so no logical record is lost before reinsertion.

3 · Reinsert 6

The old buffer is 5 and pending partner is 6, so insert 5 on the min side and 6 on the max side and link them. Pair 3↔8 remains untouched; all four remaining records are now paired, so the buffer is empty and repair stops.

Caveats

The global minimum or maximum can be the buffer. Keep correspondence pointers correct after heap swaps. Leaf correspondence is another named scheme that constrains leaves instead of pairing every node. Its repair rules differ from total correspondence.

How many records are duplicated in total correspondence?

Check your answer

None. The two queues plus the optional buffer store n records; dual heaps store two copies per record.

Course method and decision table

The course's total correspondence rule is: every record in the min priority queue has one distinct partner in the max priority queue whose key is at least as large. A buffer holds the one unpaired record when the total count is odd. Pairing is by record identity; heap positions can change independently. The paired heaps plus the optional buffer contain exactly the logical input records once.

Method or decision What to compare / change Stop condition and edge cases Evidence and cost boundary
insert(x) (course) If the buffer is empty, put x there. Otherwise compare x and the buffered record, put the smaller in the min queue and larger in the max queue, then link those identities. Empty buffer: stop after setting it. Occupied buffer: finish both heap insertions and pointer setup; equality is allowed and either identity may take either side if the invariant is retained. Lecture 3 slide 10. With binary heaps a pair insertion is O(logn); empty-buffer placement is O(1).
Repeated-insertion build Start empty and apply the course insertion rule once per input item. Finish when every input item is in a heap pair or the sole buffer; odd input leaves one buffered item, even input leaves none. This illustrated build is O(nlogn) worst case for binary heaps; lecture shows the insertion rule, not this complexity statement.
Bulk build Supplemental: divide the input into disjoint pairs; orient each pair smaller-to-min and larger-to-max; keep identity links, then bottom-up heapify both sides. Put an odd leftover in the buffer. Stop after both heap orders hold and every min record still points to its original ≥ partner. Empty and singleton lists need no heapify. Not specified in Lecture 3. With binary heaps, bottom-up construction is O(n), provided links follow identities through every swap.
peekMin / peekMax Compare the appropriate heap root with the buffer when present. Empty collection: no result. If tied, either matching record is a valid extreme; stop after returning one. Direct consequence of the two heap roots and buffer rule; O(1).
removeMin (course) If buffer is no larger than min root, remove buffer. Otherwise remove min root and its max-side partner by handle, then reinsert the partner through the buffer insertion rule. Empty: fail. Singleton buffer: return it and become empty. With equal buffer/root keys either chosen record is a minimum. Stop when the record is removed and the remaining invariant is restored. Lecture 3 slide 11 specifies the buffer-vs-root branch and partner reinsertion. Binary-heap repair makes the non-buffer case O(logn).
removeMax (symmetric course method) Mirror removeMin: compare buffer with max root; if buffer is no smaller, remove it; otherwise remove max root and its min-side partner, then reinsert that partner. Same empty/singleton/equality cases as removeMin; stop once one logical maximum is removed and all remaining records are paired or buffered. Symmetry deduction from slide 11, not a separately printed slide procedure. Binary-heap repair is O(logn).
Arbitrary partner removal Follow the cross-pointer from the selected root record to its partner. Remove that interior record from the other heap, move its last entry into the hole, then sift up if it beats its parent or down otherwise. Stop at root/ordered parent for sift-up, or leaf/ordered children for sift-down. Deleting the last slot needs no sift. Update index handles on every swap. Lecture 3 slides 5 and 7 require each single-ended queue to support arbitrary removal. Binary indexed-heap removal is O(logn) after the partner handle is known.
search(key) Supplemental: scan the stored records or use a separate identity index; heap order does not sort the full array. Stop when the desired identity/key is found or every candidate has been checked. Duplicate keys require identity if a particular record is requested. No correspondence-heap search procedure is given in Lecture 3. General scan is O(n).
meld(A,B) Supplemental rebuild: gather each logical record once, repartition into valid pairs, restore the optional buffer, and heapify both queues while preserving links. Stop only when all source records occur once and every pair, heap and buffer condition is restored. Not specified in Lecture 3. A binary-heap rebuild can be O(n+m); this is not a constant-time meld.
Leaf correspondence Treat as a separate named variant with its own leaf-only pairing and repair rules. Do not apply the total-correspondence partner invariant or its endpoint-removal explanation without that variant's rules. Lecture 3 slides 7–8 name leaf correspondence and define total correspondence; its detailed repair procedure is not supplied here.

Costs above are ordinary operation bounds for the stated binary-heap implementation where identified; they are neither amortized claims nor costs stated for every course variant. If an implementation stores array indexes in cross-handles, each swap must refresh the moved record's index; if it stores references to stable record objects, the link follows that object while the heap array entries move.

Odd counts, equal endpoints, and the buffer as an extreme

Count Representation What endpoint removal does
0 Both heaps empty; buffer empty. peekMin, peekMax, removeMin, and removeMax have no record to return.
1 One record in the buffer; both heaps empty. It is both minimum and maximum. Either removal returns it and leaves the empty structure.
2k k paired entries in each heap; buffer empty. Min and max are the two roots. Removing one endpoint deletes its pair and reinserts its partner into an empty buffer, leaving 2k−1 records.
2k+1, k≥1 k linked pairs plus one buffer entry. Compare the buffer with the relevant root; if the buffer is extreme, clear only it, otherwise remove the root pair and reinsert the partner against the buffer.

If buffer == minRoot, either is a valid minimum: removing the buffer leaves all pairs intact; choosing the root instead follows the pair-reinsertion branch. Likewise, if buffer == maxRoot, either may be returned as a maximum. A deterministic implementation can choose the buffer on equality, but it must remove exactly one record. The root comparison plus this tie rule is the stop decision; no extra repair is needed when the buffer itself wins.

For a buffer that is the global minimum, construct [10, 20, 5]: the first pair is 10↔20 and 5 is left in the buffer. removeMin returns 5 and clears the buffer; the pair remains unchanged. For a buffer that is the global maximum, [10, 20, 25] yields pair 10↔20 plus buffer 25; removeMax returns 25 and leaves that pair. These cases show why the buffer must be compared at each endpoint operation instead of treating it as automatically between the roots.

Common mistakes

  • A correspondence link joins records, not heap positions. The min and max copies are not duplicates; each logical record appears once in this structure.
  • A valid pair always points from the smaller-side record to a distinct record with an equal or larger key. Heap swaps do not change that inequality or pairing.
  • Removing a non-buffer endpoint removes its opposite-heap partner only temporarily. Reinsert that partner so only the requested endpoint disappears from the logical set.
  • The buffer can be the minimum, maximum, or equal to a heap root. Compare it to the relevant root before deciding which branch runs.
  • A min heap's root does not directly locate a key in the max heap. Use its record handle; then repair that heap after the arbitrary deletion.
  • Odd means exactly one buffer entry; even means none. The two heap sizes must match because every min-side item has exactly one max-side partner.
  • Do not apply total-correspondence steps to leaf correspondence. Lecture 3 names both variants but gives the total rule and endpoint operations on these slides.

Exam focus

  • Total correspondence: for every min-side record a, exactly one max-side record b is linked and a ≤ b.
  • With odd n, put one unpaired item in the buffer; paired queues each contain (n−1)/2 records. With even n, each contains n/2 records.
  • Insert: empty buffer → store new item; otherwise split the old buffer and new key into smaller/min and larger/max, then connect them.
  • removeMin: buffer is minimum → clear it; otherwise delete min root and linked max-side item, then reinsert the partner. removeMax is the symmetric procedure.
  • Endpoint peeks compare the appropriate root with the buffer. A singleton buffer is both extrema.
  • Lecture 3 slides 5, 7–11: linked records and arbitrary removal requirement (5, 7); odd buffer and named correspondence variants (7); total-pair inequality (8); example topology (9); insert (10); removeMin (11).
  • Do not confuse with dual heaps: dual heaps store every record twice, while total correspondence stores every record once across the two queues and optional buffer.

Practice

After inserting nine records, how many cross-pair links are present and where is the ninth record?

Check your answer

There are four links, one for each min-side record. The ninth record is the sole buffer entry. The pair heaps contain eight records total.

Why must the partner of a removed min root be deleted from the max heap before reinsertion?

Check your answer

Its old pair is being broken because the min endpoint is removed. Removing the partner frees it to be paired with the old buffer or placed in the empty buffer; leaving it in place would duplicate its logical identity and violate the one-record-once representation.

If the buffer equals the min root, must both copies be removed?

Check your answer

No. They are two distinct records with tied keys. Return and remove exactly one minimum. Choosing the buffer clears only the buffer; choosing the root uses its pair-removal and partner-reinsertion branch.
