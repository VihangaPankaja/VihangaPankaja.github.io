Red-black tree

Color constraints limit height while allowing more flexibility than AVL. Repairs use recoloring and rotations.

Rules

The root and all null/external leaves are black. A red node has a black parent. Every path from a node to an external leaf has the same number of black nodes. Keep BST ordering as well. These rules give height at most 2 log₂(n+1), under the lecture's height convention.

Fresh build from [10, 5, 1, 15]

Input list[10, 5, 1, 15]

1 · Insert 10

Insert 10 into the empty BST. Since the root must be black, color it black immediately; every root-to-external path now contains the same single internal black node. Stop because there is no parent or repair case.

2 · Insert 5 red

Compare 5 with 10: 5 < 10, so attach it as the left child and color it red. Its parent is black, so there is no red-red edge and adding red has not changed any path black-height; stop.

3 · Insert 1 red

Compare 1 < 10 and 1 < 5, then attach red 1 below red 5. The red-parent rule is violated, while black-height is unchanged. The uncle (10’s right child) is external black, so this is LLb; continue to the local repair.

4 · Rotate and recolor

Rotate right at grandparent 10: 5 is promoted, 1 remains its left child, and 10 becomes its right child. Color 5 black and 10 red. The four ordered ranges stay in order and both paths through the rotated subtree have the same black count; this is the outer black-uncle terminal repair.

5 · Insert 15 red

Compare 15 > 5, then 15 > 10, and attach it red as 10’s right child. Parent 10 is red, and uncle 1 is red, so this is XYr (here RRr). Continue by flipping parent and uncle black and grandparent temporarily red; a red grandparent can cause another violation above.

6 · Recolor

Recolor 10 and 1 black and 5 red. This preserves every path’s black count because the two child paths each gain one black while the grandparent loses one. Here 5 is the root, so force it black and stop; the root-to-external black-height is restored.

Insertion names

Insert as a red BST node. In XYz, X is the parent's side under the grandparent, Y is the inserted/current node's side under its parent, and z is the uncle's color.

Case Repair
XYr Parent and uncle black, grandparent red; continue upward
LLb / RRb Outer single rotation and recolor
LRb / RLb Inner rotation, then outer rotation and recolor

Null uncles count as black. Updating a duplicate key changes its value, without inserting another red node.

Deletion and black deficiency

If a target has two children, exchange its pair with a predecessor/successor and physically remove a node with at most one child. The color of that physically removed node controls repair. Removing red needs no black-height repair. A red replacement becomes black. A deficient root ends the repair.

For a deficient right subtree y, let p be its parent and v the left sibling. In Rbn, v is black and n counts its red children.

Lecture case Action
Rb0, p black Make v red; propagate deficiency to p
Rb0, p red Make p black and v red; stop
Rb1, outer red child Right-rotate p; transfer p's old color to v, blacken p and outer child; stop
Rb1, inner red child LR repair with recoloring; stop
Rb2 Lecture uses LR repair with recoloring; stop
Rr(0) Red sibling v, its inner child w has zero red children: lecture's LL restructuring and recoloring
Rr(1), outer red child of w LR restructuring and recoloring
Rr(1), inner red child of w Restructure through w's inner red child and recolor
Rr(2) Same through-inner-child restructuring, recolor

Rr(n) counts red children of v's inner child w, rather than children of v itself. All L cases mirror these. A useful equivalent repair strategy normalizes a red sibling first, then handles the resulting black sibling. Keep one strategy consistent while tracing.

Delete a black leaf: Rb0 with black parent

1 · Start

Begin with black root 10 and black children 5 and 15. Every route to an external leaf contains the same black nodes, and 10 has both child links, so this is the valid precondition for a black-leaf deletion trace.

2 · Physically delete 15

Physically remove black leaf 15 and retain its right child position as a black external NIL marked deficient. The right side has lost one black relative to the left; sibling 5 is black and has no red children, so this is Rb0 with black parent 10. Continue repair at parent 10.

3 · Recolor sibling 5 red

Make sibling 5 red. The sibling side loses one black, matching the deficient side; deficiency is now attached to 10. Since 10 is the root, the deficit is the whole tree’s common black-height, so stop. All root-to-NIL paths again agree.

Operations and caveats

Search, insert, delete, lowerBound and extremes cost O(logn) worst case. Build through inserts costs O(nlogn). General meld can merge inorder lists, then use a valid red-black construction; simply coloring an arbitrary balanced shape all black is not always valid. Rank/select require subtree-size metadata.

The lecture counts a whole single/double/multinode restructuring as a “rotation.” This is not a claim that every implementation uses at most one primitive left/right rotation. Color flips alone preserve topology; rotations must update parent/root pointers and any augmented fields. Do not assume all leaves containing data are black.

Why insert red instead of black?

Check your answer

A red insertion preserves black-height counts. It can create a local red-parent violation, repaired with recoloring and rotations.

Invariants and color-border reading

The border encodes node color: B is black and R is red. External NILs are black even when a diagram omits them. Check BST order, black root, no red parent-child pair, and equal black-height on every path to an external leaf. Inserting a new red leaf preserves black-height before repair; the only possible new violation is red-red. Removing a black node creates a one-black deficit at its replacement position; the color of the node physically removed decides whether repair is needed.

Deletion: choose the physically removed node

For a target with two children, copy the predecessor or successor key/value pair into the target, then physically remove that predecessor/successor (which has at most one child). Keep the physical node's color semantics separate from the copied pair. If the physically removed node was red, black-height is unchanged. If it was black and its replacement is red, color the replacement black and stop. If both are black (including NIL), carry a one-black deficiency upward.

Lecture 9 black-sibling family for a deficient right child

Let p be the parent, y the deficient right child, and v=p.left. Rb0 means v is black with zero red children; Rb1 has one red child; Rb2 has two. For the lecture's Rb1 outer-child case, rotate right at p and recolor; for the inner-child case, rotate left at v then right at p. Rb2 uses the lecture's LR restructuring and recoloring. The mirror swaps left and right. Rb0 with black p recolors v red and moves deficiency to p; with red p, blacken p and redden v and stop. Red sibling: lecture Rr(n) cases and CLRS normalization

Lecture 9 (slides 59–63) classifies the direct restructuring by n, the number of red children of w, where w=v.right in the deficient-right orientation. Rr(0) is one LL restructuring; Rr(1) has two child-position subcases; Rr(2) has both children red. The lecture performs a multi-node restructuring and recoloring directly. In contrast, the equivalent CLRS route first rotates the red sibling above p and swaps their colors, producing a black sibling; it then applies one of Rb0/Rb1/Rb2. This is the same invariant-preserving repair family, but the intermediate states and case labels are not one-to-one. Choose one route per trace; do not apply both.

For the CLRS route with a deficient right child: v is red, so rotate right at p; color v with p’s old color and make p red. The former inner child of v becomes p’s new left sibling of the deficiency. Continue with the black-sibling rules. The left-deficient mirror rotates left. This normalization has its own primitive rotation, separate from the subsequent repair rotation.

Lecture direct red-sibling cases

Keep the lecture's Rr(n) decision table distinct from the supplemental normalization above. In slides 59–63, v is the red sibling of deficient y, w is v’s inner child, and n counts w’s red children (not v’s). The named restructure redistributes the ordered ranges under the promoted node and stops after its recoloring removes the black deficit:

Lecture case (deficient right child) Source shape and action Mirror
Rr(0) w is black with no red child; LL restructuring through v, recolor, stop. Deficient left child: RR.
Rr(1), case 1 w has its outer red child; LR restructuring, recolor, stop. Swap left/right and outer side.
Rr(1), case 2 w has its inner red child; restructure through that red child, recolor, stop. Swap left/right and inner side.
Rr(2) Both children of w are red; restructure through w’s inner red child, recolor, stop. Swap left/right.

At every restructure, preserve the source’s subtree order a < b < c < d and reconnect the promoted root to the former parent (or tree root). The direct cases are one course-level restructuring event even when the pointer implementation uses multiple primitive rotations.

Root deficiency and black degree-one deletion

A black node with one real child in a valid red-black tree can only have a red child: black-height would differ if the child were black. Splice the red child into the removed node’s position and blacken it. If the deficient replacement is the root, there is no parent case to inspect; blacken a red root if present, otherwise accept the whole tree’s reduced black-height and stop. Method decisions and costs

Method Decision / procedure Stop condition and edge cases Cost
Search(key) Ordinary BST comparison; equality returns pair, smaller/larger selects left/right. Stop on equality or NIL; a missing key returns absent. O(logn) worst case
Insert / put Search insertion position; update value on duplicate. Otherwise attach a red leaf and repair XYr or XYb. Black parent ends immediately; root forced black; NIL uncle is black. O(logn) worst case
Update existing pair Replace value only; no topology or color change. Equality must not create a duplicate key. O(logn) worst case
Delete(key) For two children copy predecessor/successor pair; physically splice a node with at most one child; use its color. Red removal ends; red replacement becomes black; black/NIL replacement invokes deficiency repair; deficient root ends. Missing key changes nothing. O(logn) worst case
lowerBound(x) Track smallest candidate key ≥ x while following BST comparisons. Return candidate or none; equality is itself the answer. O(logn) worst case
minimum / maximum Follow left / right links to the extreme real node. Empty tree returns none. O(logn) worst case
inorder / range Inorder visits sorted pairs; range traversal prunes subtrees outside bounds. Stop traversal after keys exceed upper endpoint; empty result is valid. O(n) full traversal; O(logn+k) range with k outputs
Build from n pairs Repeated insertion, including duplicate-update policy. A sorted bulk build is possible only with a separately valid red-black coloring/construction. Empty input gives empty tree; do not color an arbitrary balanced shape all black. O(nlogn) repeated inserts
General meld Inorder both trees, merge unique-key sequences according to duplicate policy, then construct and color a valid red-black tree. Disjoint ordered-range join is a separate specialized method. O(n+m) after traversal/rebuild
rank / select (augmented) Maintain subtree sizes; descend by left-subtree size for select and accumulate skipped sizes for rank. Requires metadata and rotation updates; not native in an unaugmented tree. O(logn) with subtree-size augmentation

Worst-case bounds follow from logarithmic height. A single deletion can inspect O(logn) ancestors. The lecture’s “at most one rotation” means one restructuring event, which can be a double or multi-node restructuring; it does not promise at most one primitive left/right pointer rotation. Color flips preserve topology. Every primitive rotation must reconnect the former parent or root and update augmented fields.

Supplemental distinction

The direct lecture Rr(n) restructuring is the course’s named red-sibling algorithm. The separately labeled CLRS normalization is a standard equivalent invariant repair route, not a renaming of the lecture’s subcases. No alternative insertion/deletion algorithm is assumed by the method table.
