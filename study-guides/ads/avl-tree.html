AVL tree

Keep the two subtree heights within one at every node. Rotations restore that rule while preserving sorted order.

Rule

Balance factor bf(x)=h(left)−h(right), and every node must have bf ∈ {−1,0,1}. Use height(empty)=0 and height(leaf)=1 consistently. Minimum node counts satisfy N₀=0, N₁=1, Nₕ=1+Nₕ₋₁+Nₕ₋₂, giving logarithmic height.

The empty root is the insertion search result, so 30 becomes the only node; height is 1, bf is 0, the BST order holds, and insertion retracing ends at the root.

Since 10 < 30, attach it as the left child; subtree heights 1 and 0 give bf(30)=+1, still legal, so no rotation is needed.

Left rotation promotes 20 above 10 and transfers 20's former left subtree to 10-right; it is empty here. Inorder is preserved, but the outer repair at 30 remains necessary.

After ordinary BST insertion, retrace toward the root. bf becoming 0 stops height propagation. At bf=±2, rebalance the nearest ancestor. One insertion needs at most one rebalancing event, which can contain two primitive rotations. A rotation preserves inorder by moving the middle subtree to the other side of the lowered node.

Deletion cases

Delete as in a BST, then retrace from the parent of the physically removed node. Deleting from the left decreases bf; deleting from the right increases bf. New bf=±1 stops height propagation, bf=0 continues, and ±2 requires repair.

For deletion from A's right subtree, A becomes bf=+2 and B=A.left:

Name bf(B) Repair Continue upward?
R0 0 Right-rotate A No: repaired subtree height unchanged from before deletion
R1 +1 Right-rotate A Yes: height falls by 1
R−1 −1 LR double rotation Yes: height falls by 1

L0, L−1 and L1 are the mirrors after deleting on the left. For LR/RL, final child balance factors depend on the middle node's old bf. Recomputing heights avoids guessing them.

Operations, caveats and exam focus

Search, insert, delete, lowerBound and extremes are O(logn) worst case. A deletion may rebalance O(logn) ancestors. Build an unsorted list through inserts in O(nlogn); sorted medians permit O(n) construction. General meld by inorder merge and balanced rebuild is O(n+m). Rank/select need size augmentation.

Never infer the deletion repair from the deleted key alone. Use the current child balance. Update the root, parent links, heights and any leftSize fields after rotations. AVL is a search tree, so arbitrary general meld cannot use the heap's simple compare-root rule.

Why can deletion need several rotations when insertion stops after one event?

Check your answer

Deletion can shorten the repaired subtree, making its parent unbalanced too. Insertion repair restores the subtree’s old height.

Rotation mechanics

For every rotation, reconnect the promoted node to the old parent or root. A right rotation moves the promoted node's right subtree to the lowered node's left; a left rotation moves the promoted node's left subtree to the lowered node's right. Key ranges stay ordered. Recompute the lowered node's height first, then the promoted node's height. The path names the direction from the first unbalanced ancestor A toward the inserted key.

Lookup and insertion edge cases

An empty-tree insertion installs a bf=0 root. A duplicate dictionary key updates its value without adding a node or changing heights. Exact search stops on equality or a null child; a missing key returns not-found without mutation. After a new leaf is attached, bf 0 means subtree height did not grow and retracing stops; ±1 means it grew and retracing may continue; ±2 triggers repair at the nearest ancestor. Insertion repair restores the pre-insert subtree height. Always reconnect the promoted node to its parent or root; update parent pointers and optional metadata if the implementation stores them.

Deletion cases and height continuation

If A loses height on its right, bf(A) rises to +2 and B=A.left. B's current factor, not the deleted key, selects repair. Deletion on A's left mirrors the links and signs: L0/L−1 use a left rotation; L1 uses right then left.

After a continued repair, move to the old parent. A new parent bf of 0 means its height fell and retracing continues; ±1 means its height did not change and retracing stops; ±2 requires another repair. Therefore deletion may rebalance O(logn) ancestors. For LR/RL, recompute child heights after rotation; final factors depend on the middle node's previous factor.

For deletion edge cases, unlink a leaf and retrace from its former parent; deleting the only root leaf yields an empty tree. For a one-child node, splice its child into place; deleting the root promotes that child. For two children, copy/move the inorder successor or predecessor, physically remove that replacement node, and retrace from the physical removal's parent. A missing key causes no height change or retracing. These are standard BST deletion reductions; Lecture 9 focuses on AVL retracing and repair after removal.

Method decision table

Lecture 9 names get/search, put/insert, remove, ascend, get(index) and remove(index) as dictionary operations; AVL slides detail insertion and deletion. Ordered helpers follow the BST invariant. Supplemental rows below are not additional AVL algorithms taught in Lecture 9.

Method Decision / procedure Stop condition and edge cases Cost
get(key) / search Compare and follow left/right links. Stop at equality or null; missing key returns not-found without mutation. O(logn) worst
put(key,value) / insert Update a duplicate or attach a new leaf; retrace, then repair nearest ±2 ancestor. Duplicate does not change shape; empty tree installs root; insertion stops when height stops growing or after repair. O(logn) worst
update(key,value) Update existing key; some APIs define absent update as not-found, while put inserts. Follow the API contract; absent update-only must not create a key. O(logn) worst
remove(key) / delete Reduce two-child delete to physical successor/predecessor removal, splice node/child, then retrace. Missing key is a no-op; root leaf empties tree; root with one child promotes child; R0/L0 stop. O(logn) worst
findMin / minimum Follow left pointers. Stop at leftmost key; empty tree has no minimum. O(logn) worst
findMax / maximum Follow right pointers. Stop at rightmost key; empty tree has no maximum. O(logn) worst
lowerBound(x) Save a candidate when key ≥ x and continue left; otherwise go right. Equality qualifies; no result if x exceeds maximum. O(logn) worst
ascend() / inorder Traverse left, node, right. Empty tree returns empty sequence; BST invariant gives sorted output. O(n)
range(a,b) Prune keys outside the interval and emit matches in order. Specify inclusive endpoints; empty/reversed range yields no matches. Supplemental ordered query. O(logn+k)
build(records) Repeated AVL inserts, or median construction from sorted records with bottom-up heights. Apply duplicate policy consistently; do not claim an unbalanced sequential BST build. O(nlogn); O(n) from sorted input
meld(T1,T2) General overlapping meld: inorder, merge by key policy, rebuild balanced. A specialized join needs disjoint ranges and height-aware logic; supplemental, not specified by Lecture 9. O(n+m) rebuild
get(index) / remove(index) Add subtree-size metadata and update it on insert/delete/rotation. Reject negative/out-of-range indexes; AVL height alone does not support ranks. Supplemental. O(logn) with metadata

AVL operations are worst-case logarithmic due to the lecture's height bound; inorder is linear. Building by repeated inserts is O(nlogn); general merge-and-rebuild is O(n+m). These are not expected or amortized claims. Lecture 9 gives log2(n+1)≤h≤1.44log2(n+2).

Common mistakes and exam focus

  • This sign convention is left height minus right height; negative means right-heavy.
  • Name insertion from the path to the new key; name deletion from the shortened side and current heavy-child factor.
  • Preserve the transferred middle subtree and reconnect parent/root links on every rotation.
  • After two-child deletion, retrace from the physically removed successor/predecessor position.
  • Insertion has at most one rebalancing event; deletion can repair O(logn) ancestors. R0/L0 stop because height is restored.
  • General overlapping meld is not heap-style root linking. Rank/select require explicit subtree-size metadata.
